## Part A — exploration

**Budget: about 450 words, 600 hard ceiling.** Push past the theme's first obvious reading, but do it briskly. No preamble, no restating the theme back at me.

- **Angles:** exactly three directions for the pack, **one or two sentences each**, differing in style and in what the pack leads with. Then name your pick in a single sentence saying why it beats the other two.
- **The asset list:** a numbered list of every item in the pack, one short line each, grouped by category — characters, objects, fillers. Stay inside your total count. Make them theme-native rather than the default balloon-cake-present trio.
- **Cohesion:** one or two lines fixing the shared outline weight, corner radius, face style, and simplification level that tie the set together.
- **Palette:** one line — dominant, supports, accent, with named colours used across every asset.
- **Scale fixes:** a short bullet list only — anything to enlarge, simplify, or delete so each item survives at small print size. Just the fixes, not the reasoning.

Skip anything not on that list: no tradeoff essays, no runner-up post-mortems, no notes on your own process.

## Part B — the final prompt

End your response with the final image-generation prompt for the chosen pack, and nothing after it.

Format it as a single fenced code block labelled `FINAL IMAGE PROMPT`, so it can be copied cleanly. Use the labelled-block template from step 1 in order: Format → Concept → Assets → Layout → Line & fill → Palette → Background → Constraints last.

**Length: 180–250 words.** Long enough to carry the full spec, short enough that no single instruction gets diluted. If you are over, cut assets before you cut any layout rule — fewer, larger, cleanly separated items beat a crowded sheet.

Hard requirements for that prompt:

- Its **first sentence** states this is a **single square asset sheet of separate clip-art items in flat vector style on a plain flat white background**. Opening words set the mode, and turning this into a scene is the failure it is most prone to.
- It lists the assets explicitly as a numbered list rather than saying "a set of party graphics".
- It states the **grid layout with even spacing, nothing overlapping or touching, and nothing cropped at the sheet edge**.
- It gives the shared outline weight and the named palette applied across every asset.
- The exclusion list comes **last**, after every positive descriptor, and leads with no scene, no background setting, no overlapping items, no shared ground line or shadow.
- It is self-contained: someone pasting it cold, with no memory of this conversation, gets the same image. No "as described above", no "the assets from earlier".
- One prompt only. No variants, no alternates, no commentary after the code block.
