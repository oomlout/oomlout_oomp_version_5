## Part A — exploration

**Budget: about 450 words, 600 hard ceiling.** Push past the theme's first obvious reading, but do it briskly. No preamble, no restating the theme back at me.

- **Angles:** exactly three framing directions, **one or two sentences each**, differing in structure rather than just ornament. Then name your pick in a single sentence saying why it beats the other two.
- **The element list:** a numbered list of every piece in the family, one short line each, saying what each is for. Stay inside your count. Make the ornament theme-native rather than default party borders.
- **Text zones:** one line per element giving where the zone sits and roughly how much of the element it occupies. Confirm in a single sentence that all zones ship completely blank.
- **Cohesion:** one or two lines fixing shared border weight, corner logic, ornament vocabulary, and the named palette.
- **Scale fixes:** a short bullet list only — anything to thicken, enlarge, simplify, or delete so the elements survive both small print and large signage. Just the fixes, not the reasoning.

Skip anything not on that list: no tradeoff essays, no runner-up post-mortems, no notes on your own process.

## Part B — the final prompt

End your response with the final image-generation prompt for the chosen family, and nothing after it.

Format it as a single fenced code block labelled `FINAL IMAGE PROMPT`, so it can be copied cleanly. Use the labelled-block template from step 1 in order: Format → Concept → Elements → Text zones → Layout → Line & fill → Palette → Background → Constraints last.

**Length: 180–250 words.** Long enough to carry the full spec, short enough that no single instruction gets diluted. If you are over, cut elements or ornament before you cut any text-zone rule.

Hard requirements for that prompt:

- Its **first sentence** states this is a **single square sheet of separate blank banner and frame elements in flat vector style on a plain flat white background**.
- It says **every text zone is left completely empty** — and says it twice, once in the Text zones block and once in the exclusions, because inventing text is the failure this design is most prone to.
- It lists the elements explicitly as a numbered list.
- It states the **grid layout with even spacing, nothing overlapping or touching, nothing cropped at the edge**.
- It gives the shared border weight and the named palette, with light flat interiors inside the text zones.
- The exclusion list comes **last**, after every positive descriptor, and **leads with the text group**: no text anywhere, no words, letters, numbers, names, dates, placeholder or lorem text, and no squiggles standing in for text.
- It is self-contained: someone pasting it cold, with no memory of this conversation, gets the same image. No "as described above", no "the elements from earlier".
- One prompt only. No variants, no alternates, no commentary after the code block.
