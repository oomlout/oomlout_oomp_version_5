## The product this style serves

**Intended use:** a single hero image of one chibi character, rendered to look like a **premium art-directed 3D collectible** — the polish of a designer vinyl figure or a high-end animated feature, not a flat illustration and not a photoreal creature.

**Target feel:** huggable, joyful, a little mischievous. Instantly readable at thumbnail size. Full colour, clean studio-style presentation, character as the unmistakable focus.

**The failure mode to design against:** generic stock mascot — a symmetrical smiling blob standing front-on against a gradient, with plastic-looking everything and no idea of its own.

## What to produce

Six sections. **Budget: about 900 words total, 1,100 hard ceiling** — roughly 100–150 words each, section 6 allowed the most. This is a working reference, not an essay. Tight bullets, numbers wherever a number applies, and cut any sentence that restates this brief back at me.

**1. Chibi proportion system**
The specific numbers that make chibi read as cute rather than deformed: head-to-body ratio range, eye size and spacing as a fraction of head width, limb length and thickness, hand and foot simplification level, where the centre of mass sits. State the ranges that work and the ones that tip into uncanny.

**2. Face and expression**
The small set of levers that carry emotional appeal — eye shape and highlight placement, brow presence, mouth simplicity, cheek and blush restraint, head tilt. One line each. Then a short do-not list: dead-eyed symmetrical stares, over-detailed irises, realistic teeth, aggressive or smug expressions, and the fix for each.

**3. Pose and silhouette**
How to get a pose that feels alive rather than catalogued: weight shift, gesture line, asymmetry, one clear action beat, a prop held rather than floated. Plus the silhouette test — what to check so major forms stay separate and readable when the image is 100 px wide.

**4. Material and lighting menu**
This is the main lever for premium CGI feel, so be concrete. Give a named menu of surfaces worth calling for — soft-touch matte vinyl, glossy PVC, satin fabric, brushed metal, rubberised accents, soft translucent resin, flocked or felted texture — and note what each contributes and where it goes wrong. Then the lighting recipe: soft key, gentle fill, subtle rim separation, clean controlled speculars, soft contact shadow to ground the figure. Say why hard shadows, heavy grain, and dramatic contrast all cheapen this style.

Also flag the vocabulary trap: words like *photorealistic*, *real photograph*, and *taken on a real camera* engage the model's photoreal mode and will drag the character toward unsettling realism. The cues that actually land this style are **stylized 3D render**, **premium designer vinyl collectible**, **soft studio lighting**, and named materials.

**5. Colour and background**
Palette discipline — a dominant colour, one or two supports, one accent for the eye to land on. Value separation between character and background. Background stays a clean studio sweep or soft gradient with nothing competing. Say how much colour saturation is joyful versus garish.

**6. Prompt architecture for step 2**
The most important section, so spend your words here. Specify the exact template the step-2 final prompt must follow. Use **short labelled blocks**, not one long paragraph — that is the shape that holds up for character work:

```
Concept:      one line on the idea and the mood
Character:    species/subject, proportions, face, expression, pose, costume or markings
Details:      props and supporting motifs, kept few
Style:        stylized 3D render, named materials, surface finishes
Camera:       framing, aspect, angle, focal feel, character centred
Lighting:     key, fill, rim, contact shadow
Background:   clean studio treatment
Constraints:  exclusions, LAST
```

State that the finished prompt should land at **180–250 words**, and that exclusions always come last, after every positive descriptor — a model reading negatives early tends to treat them as things to compose rather than things to omit.

Give the canonical exclusion list to close with: no photorealism, no realistic human anatomy, no creepy or uncanny facial proportions, no harsh shadows, no heavy film grain or noise, no busy scenery, no clutter, no tiny unreadable detail, no muddy or muddled textures, no text, no watermark, no signature, no logos or trademarks, no extra characters.

Also state the two things the prompt must say in plain words no matter what: it is a **fun chibi CGI-style render**, and the result should read as **polished and premium**.
