## The product this style serves

**Intended use:** a set of birthday **cake toppers and party cutouts** — compact pieces that get printed, cut out, mounted on sticks or picks, and pushed into a cake or dessert-table display. Full colour, flat vector, bold outer silhouettes.

**Critical: this produces ONE image containing MANY pieces.** The output is a single square **cutout sheet** — separate pieces arranged on a grid against plain flat white, generously spaced so a blade can get between them. Not a cake, not a scene, not a photo of a decorated dessert table.

**Everything here gets cut out by hand or machine**, so silhouette sturdiness matters more than detail. A piece that looks charming on screen and snaps off at the neck is a failed piece.

**The failure mode to design against:** clip art on sticks — nice drawings with fragile edges, thin necks, and shapes nobody can cut.

## What to produce

Six sections. **Budget: about 900 words total, 1,100 hard ceiling** — roughly 100–150 words each, section 6 allowed the most. Tight bullets, numbers wherever a number applies, and cut any sentence that restates this brief back at me.

**1. Topper categories**
The piece types worth including: age or number toppers, short-word toppers, mini mascots, themed objects, and small filler picks. Give a working set size and a recommended mix, noting which categories carry the set. State a total that keeps each piece large on the sheet.

**2. Cut-safety rules**
The heart of this brief, so be concrete. One bold closed outer contour per piece with a white keyline border. Give minimum limb and neck thickness as a fraction of piece height, minimum concave gap so a blade can turn, the ban on isolated floating islands, the rule for merging fiddly appendages into the body, and the minimum radius on any inside corner.

**3. Stick and mount logic**
What makes a topper mountable: a solid, unbroken area at the base of each piece wide and tall enough to take a stick, no delicate detail in the bottom band, and pieces built so their weight sits above the mount rather than off to one side.

**4. Silhouette and small-format readability**
Why the outer shape has to carry the piece from a metre away, minimum feature size as a fraction of piece height, sparse interior detail, and what to merge or delete. Note that these are seen against a busy cake, so value contrast at the contour matters more than internal refinement.

**5. Text, cohesion and palette**
If text is used it must be short, heavy, and fully enclosed by its own shape or backing panel — never thin free-floating letters, which are the single most common cut failure. Give a maximum character count and letter weight. Then cohesion: shared outline weight, shared corner radius, shared face style, and a shared palette with a dominant / support / accent split and a working colour count.

**6. Prompt architecture for step 2**
The most important section, so spend your words here. Specify the exact template the step-2 final prompt must follow. Use **short labelled blocks**, not one long paragraph:

```
Format:       single square sheet of separate cutout pieces, flat vector style
Concept:      one line on the theme and mood
Pieces:       a numbered list, one line each, with any quoted text
Layout:       grid, even spacing, nothing overlapping or touching
Cut spec:     bold closed contour, white keyline border, thick necks, solid base
Line & fill:  shared outline weight, flat fills, sparse interiors
Palette:      named colours shared across every piece
Background:   plain flat white behind all pieces
Constraints:  exclusions, LAST
```

State that the finished prompt should land at **180–250 words**, and that exclusions always come last, after every positive descriptor — a model reading negatives early tends to treat them as things to compose rather than things to omit.

Give the canonical exclusion list to close with: no cake, no dessert table, no scene or background setting, no photograph or 3D render, no sticks or skewers drawn in, no pieces overlapping or touching, nothing cropped at the edge, no thin protrusions, no isolated floating pieces, no drop shadows, no gradients, no texture or raster noise, no extra text beyond what is quoted, no watermark, no signature.

Also state the two things the prompt must say in plain words no matter what: it is a **single flat sheet of separate cutout pieces on a plain flat white background**, and every piece has a **bold closed outer contour with a white keyline border**.
